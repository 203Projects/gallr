## Problem Statement

Owner's words, verbatim: "look into latest mobile version and look for improvement plans. i don't think ai feature is feeling fully ai and accurate. come up with plan to make this better and appealing for users."

Restated: gallr 1.14.0 (Android 43 approved, iOS 33 in review on 2026-10-21) has one thing readers would call AI, 내 취향 / FOR YOU, and one thing that is AI-made but reads as editorial, 에디터의 시선. 내 취향 is an on-device, hand-weighted ranker over character n-grams and a 28-word keyword list. It never asks the visitor anything, never takes a correction, and explains itself in one template line. It is starved: in the 2026-10-02 catalogue snapshot 73 of 78 exhibitions have no artist credit and 74 of 78 have no reviewed art term (spec 088 measured 5 of 71 and 4 of 71). So the "taste" it finds is mostly shared wording, and the reasons it gives ("저장한 X와 비슷한 전시") are true but thin. A first-time visitor sees editorial picks relabelled 내 취향. That is why it feels neither accurate nor like AI.

The two halves of the complaint have two different causes: accuracy is a data problem; "feels AI" is a dialogue problem. The plan treats them in that order.

## Status Quo

Outside gallr: Instagram, Naver blog, KakaoTalk tips and posters (recorded in the 2026-04 designs). Inside gallr: the List tab and the Map tab, with 내 취향 as a six-card rail on Home and a list behind 모두 보기. The only lever a visitor has is the save heart; saved and visited items leave the pool on the next recompute, which is the opposite of what a visitor who just saved something expects to see.

What the code does today (static read of `LocalExhibitionRecommender.kt`, `TasteProfile.kt`, `HomeSituations.kt`, `RecommendationPresentation.kt`, `LocalDiscoveryViewModel.kt`):

- Signals: saved and visited exhibitions as anchors, followed galleries, featured, editor pick, closing soon. Weights: shared reviewed artist 0.50/0.35, reviewed term 0.35/0.25, keyword-detected term 0.08/0.06 (rarity-scaled, only a reason above 0.15), text similarity 0.20/0.12 (character 2–3-gram TF-IDF cosine, venue boilerplate and ubiquitous n-grams removed, similar-pair share capped at 10% by spec 088), followed gallery 0.18, featured 0.08, editor 0.05, closing 0.05. Proximity exists (0.20) but the For You screen passes no origin, so it never fires there.
- Ranking: candidates with any personal evidence first, then score, then id; max two per gallery; near-duplicate cap; zero-evidence candidates dropped; six cards; two reasons each, strongest anchor named.
- Cold start: only featured, editor and closing-soon survive. Basis line: "전시를 저장하면 취향에 맞춰 골라드려요".
- Taste chips: top four terms by anchor count, two per category, not tappable, no rarity weighting (회화 can lead the chips while being barred as a reason).
- Situations (상황별·주제별): six static term bundles; themes data-driven from terms held by 3 to N/3 exhibitions.
- No ML, no embeddings, no persistence, no learning, no feedback control, no onboarding, no reason on the detail page, no accuracy metric (no precision@k), 24 + 15 + 8 hand-picked assertions plus the ≤10% similarity bound.
- 에디터의 시선: 40 approved notes, desk-drafted (Claude Code session, no API key in production), deterministic grounding checks, approved by a person in Admin, no byline (owner decision 2026-10-13).

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

Target user (advisor reading, to be confirmed): the visitor who has saved three or more exhibitions. They are the only people for whom 내 취향 can be right today, and they are the ones who notice when it is not. Second: the first-time visitor who opens Home and sees "내 취향" over picks that have nothing to do with them.

Narrowest wedge: a 20-second taste pick on first open, every card's reason naming the visitor's own pick or save, a 덜 보기 control, all running on metadata that exists for every running exhibition instead of 6% of them. No model is needed for the wedge. The wedge ships in the next store release and is the first half of the recommended approach.

## Premises

Each needs the owner's agree/disagree. They were not confirmed live.

1. The complaint is about 내 취향 first. 에디터의 시선 already reads as editorial and stays as it is. — agree/disagree?
2. The accuracy ceiling is metadata coverage, not the algorithm. With artists and terms on 6% of the catalogue, no ranking method can give specific reasons; the first investment is tagging every running and upcoming exhibition, using the desk pipeline that already works for notes. — agree/disagree?
3. "Feels AI" means it listens first: the visitor can tell it their taste (a first-open pick, a 덜 보기 control, editable chips) and every pick is explained by naming what it knows about them. Whether it also needs to talk back in generated sentences is unproven; Codex's cold read (below) argues relevant choices plus visible control may be enough. Revised reading: the voice (Stage 2) is built only if the Stage 1 observation shows visitors still do not feel known. It does not mean a chat window and it does not mean saying "AI". — agree/disagree?
4. No paid service. The platforms' free on-device models may be used where they exist, behind an interface with a template fallback, so the feature is complete on every device and better on some. — agree/disagree?
5. Taste stays on the device. No new analytics field, no sync, no server-side profile. The taste seed is local like bookmarks were before account sync. — agree/disagree?
6. Success is measured: an offline eval on a labelled catalogue fixture in `commonTest` before release, and saves-from-For-You once analytics collection is enabled, not how a demo feels. — agree/disagree?

Landscape (in-distribution knowledge; the problem-space web search was skipped because the privacy gate could not be answered; two platform-documentation lookups were run and are cited in Constraints):

- Layer 1, what everyone knows: a recommender over about 70 live items is a metadata and editorial problem, not a model problem; cold start is solved with an interest picker (Letterboxd, Pinterest, Spotify all ask); "Because you watched" explanations build trust; silent recommenders are distrusted.
- Layer 2, current discourse: to consumers in 2025–26 "AI" means it talks in sentences and adapts when corrected; readers punish generic AI copy, which the owner already acted on by removing the byline; both phone platforms now ship a free on-device language model with structured output.
- Layer 3, where conventional wisdom is wrong here: EUREKA. Everyone adds a hosted model because they assume the AI feel comes from the model. In gallr 95% of items carry no structured metadata, so any model would rank on noise and explain with boilerplate. The bottleneck is data and dialogue, both free. The model is the last thing to add, and when it is added it should only rephrase evidence the deterministic engine already produced, exactly as the notes pipeline only writes what the packet says.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Data and dialogue, no model ("make it accurate")

Summary: tag the whole running catalogue at the desk, widen the vocabulary, add a first-open taste pick, a 덜 보기 control, editable chips, reasons that name the visitor's own choices, and an offline accuracy eval. No language model.
Effort: M (human ~4 weeks / CC ~4 days plus the owner's tagging approvals)
Risk: Low. Everything reuses the notes desk pattern, the Admin metadata editor and the existing evidence model.
Completeness: 7/10 (accurate and responsive; still speaks in templates)
Pros: fixes the half of the complaint that is measurable; ships in 1.15 with no new dependency; every device gets all of it.
Cons: still no sentence that sounds like it knows you; the first-open pick is the only "AI moment"; the owner may still feel it is "not fully AI".
Reuses: `.claude/skills/curator-notes` desk pattern, `admin/src/components/ExhibitionArtMetadataEditor.tsx`, `admin_search_artists` / `admin_create_artist`, `ExhibitionEvidence` kinds in `LocalExhibitionRecommender.kt`, `TasteProfile.tasteTerms`, DataStore preference pattern, `DiscoveryFixture`.

### Approach B: A plus an on-device voice ("make it accurate, then make it speak") — recommended

Summary: Approach A first, then a `TasteNarrator` seam with the platforms' free on-device models (Foundation Models on iOS 26 Apple Intelligence devices, ML Kit GenAI Prompt API on supported Androids) and a template implementation everywhere else. The narrator only rephrases structured evidence the ranker produced: a two-sentence personal line at the top of 내 취향, a natural-language ask in the List tab search field that maps to existing filters, and later a weekly digest card. Output is validated like a curator note (every exhibition name, artist and term must appear in the evidence packet; a Korean sentence must contain Hangul and any Latin run in it must be a substring of the packet; an English sentence may contain Hangul only as a substring of the packet; length caps) and discarded on failure.
Effort: L (human ~10 weeks / CC ~2 weeks plus device verification on iPhone 15 Pro+ and Pixel 9 or Galaxy S25)
Risk: Medium. Korean quality of the on-device models is unverified; device coverage is a minority at first; the iOS API is Swift-only so the bridge lives in `iosApp` and is passed into `MainViewController` like the other host parameters; Prompt API is alpha.
Completeness: 9/10
Pros: delivers both halves of the complaint; zero cost; nothing leaves the device so the privacy line "이 기기에서만 계산" stays literally true; the fallback is Approach A, so there is no device on which the feature is broken.
Cons: two store releases, not one; the best version is seen by a minority of devices until the platforms widen; needs a verification matrix the CI cannot run.
Reuses: all of A, the notes pipeline's grounding-check idea (`validateDraft`), the platform-shim pattern (`ShareHandler`, `NotificationScheduler`), `ReleaseFeatures`, the List tab filter model.

### Approach C: Editorial taste types ("make it feel designed")

Summary: six to eight staff-written 취향 유형 (for example 고요한 미니멀리스트, 사진 산책자, 낯선 감각 탐험가), each a term bundle plus a desk-written weekly paragraph; a five-question quiz assigns a type on first open; 내 취향 becomes the type's bundle intersected with the catalogue plus the visitor's saves; a shareable type card for Instagram.
Effort: S–M (human ~3 weeks / CC ~3 days)
Risk: Low to build; medium that it reads as a quiz gimmick rather than a curator.
Completeness: 6/10 (appealing and shareable; "AI" is theatrical; accuracy still needs the same tagging as A)
Pros: strong share hook; uses the notes desk and the carousel export; no runtime model at all.
Cons: everyone of a type sees the same picks; a type does not learn from saves; it competes with the real taste signal gallr already has (bookmarks).
Reuses: `HomeSituations` bundles, the notes desk, the Instagram export.

Ruled out during research:
- A hosted model (Claude, Gemini, free tiers included): violates the no-API-key rule and the 071/074 boundary; a free tier is a paid tier with a delay.
- Bundling a small open model (Gemma-class through LiteRT) in the app: free but 0.5–2 GB on every install for a 70-item catalogue; revisit only if the platform models' coverage stays small after a year.
- Sentence embeddings for text similarity: adds a 100 MB model to beat n-grams on 70 items when the real gap is tags; not now.

### Stage 1 — Listening (release 1.15)

4. First-open taste pick: when the basis is empty, no seed is stored and the sheet has not been skipped, Home shows a sheet, "어떤 전시가 끌리세요?" / "What draws you?", with eight to ten term chips (terms held by ≥3 running exhibitions) and six featured covers; pick three or more, or skip. Stored in DataStore as a local seed (term ids and exhibition ids), like bookmarks before account sync; no account asked.
   - Skip: a `taste_pick_skipped` flag is stored and the sheet never shows on its own again; it opens only from the 취향 고르기 button. After a skip, or with no seed and no basis, the Home section under the heading 내 취향 추천 shows one prompt card (Home's 16dp card, hairline outline, like the update nudge): "전시를 고르거나 저장하면 취향에 맞춰 골라드려요" / "Pick or save exhibitions and the picks follow your taste", with the button 취향 고르기 / PICK YOUR TASTE. Editorial picks stay under 이번 주 추천 only; nothing editorial is shown under a taste heading.
   - Chosen terms: new anchor source `Chosen` in `LocalExhibitionRecommender`. A chosen term matches a candidate's reviewed term at 0.30 (between reviewed-term-via-save 0.35 and detected-term 0.08) and a candidate's detected term at 0.08, rarity-scaled as today. Reason: "내가 고른 #미니멀" / "YOUR PICK · #MINIMAL".
   - Chosen covers: a chosen exhibition is an anchor like a save, with the same derived evidence (shared reviewed artist, title artist, reviewed term, detected term, text similarity) at 0.6× the saved weights (artist 0.30, title artist 0.24, term 0.21, detected 0.05, text 0.12), combined by the same noisy-OR, and it leaves the candidate pool like a save. Reasons name it: 내가 고른 "X"와 같은 작가: A / YOUR PICK "X" · SAME ARTIST: A; 내가 고른 "X"와 공통 주제: T / YOUR PICK "X" · SHARED THEME: T; 내가 고른 "X"와 비슷한 전시 / YOUR PICK "X" · SIMILAR EXHIBITION.
   - The 내 취향 chips become buttons that open the same sheet to edit. Chip rule, independent of score arithmetic: a term with at least one standing dismissal (item 5) is excluded from the four chips and listed in the sheet's 덜 보기 row with an undo per term; undoing restores it to chip selection by anchor count as today.
5. 덜 보기 / "Less like this" in each For You card's overflow: hides that exhibition until its closing date and adds a negative anchor for each of its reviewed terms and credited artists, with a 되돌리기 snackbar. Stored locally in one DataStore value, `taste_dismissals`: one record per dismissed exhibition holding its id, its closing date, and the reviewed term ids and credited artist ids it carried at dismissal time. Per-id dismissal counts are derived by counting the standing records that contain the id, so a count is never below zero and a later metadata edit cannot shift which ids a record touches. Each reversal path is one operation on those records: the 되돌리기 snackbar deletes that exhibition's record (each of its ids loses one count); saving, visiting or following a hidden exhibition or its gallery deletes that exhibition's record the same way (the lift); the sheet's per-term undo removes that term id from every standing record, which clears the term's count, while the records themselves stand so the exhibitions stay hidden until their closing dates (the visitor hid the show, not only the term). Negative evidence, defined once in ranker terms:
   - Per id penalty = min(0.25 × dismissal count, 0.5 × BASE), where BASE is a constant per id kind independent of the candidate: 0.35 for a term id (the reviewed-term-via-save weight) and 0.50 for an artist id (the reviewed-artist-via-save weight). So one dismissal costs 0.175 on a term and 0.25 on an artist, and further dismissals of the same id add nothing.
   - Per candidate, the penalty is the sum over the candidate's reviewed term ids and credited artist ids that have dismissals (detected terms and title artists are not penalised). It is subtracted from the candidate's score after all positive evidence has been combined by noisy-OR and summed, and the result is floored at zero. The candidate keeps its tier: a candidate with personal evidence stays in the personal-evidence tier and is only lowered within it; an editorial-only candidate is lowered within the editorial tier. A dismissal therefore never moves a card out of 내 취향 기반 and never flips a save, visit, follow or chosen signal; it can lower any candidate carrying the dismissed id, including one that also carries a chosen or saved term.
   - A lift (save, visit or follow) and a per-term undo both return the term to chip eligibility, because both leave the term with a derived count of zero.
6. Reasons name the visitor's own choice first, and editorial never fills a taste slot. On Home, once any personal evidence exists (seed, save, visit or follow), the 내 취향 rail shows only cards with personal evidence, from one to six; if none qualifies the prompt card of item 4 shows instead. The 모두 보기 list keeps its two sections, 내 취향 기반 and 이번 주 볼 만한 전시, so editorial backfill stays visible but never under a taste reason (FR-017 and FR-022 unchanged). Tier order for the two reasons: Chosen anchors first, then Saved, then the existing kind order within an anchor (artist, term, text); still two reasons, still the strongest anchor named.
7. Show the reason on the detail page when opened from 내 취향 (the attribution the analytics path already carries), under the title in `labelSmall`, so the explanation survives the tap.
8. Offline accuracy eval in `shared/commonTest`: `TasteEvalFixture` with at least fifteen scenarios the owner labels (a seed or save set and the expected top three) over the published fixture; assert precision@3 ≥ 0.6 and that every shown reason is grounded. Also assert the dismissal arithmetic of item 5: one dismissal lowers a candidate carrying the term by exactly 0.175 and keeps it in the personal tier when it has personal evidence; a second dismissal of the same term changes nothing; a candidate's score never goes below zero; a dismissed term is absent from the chips and present in the 덜 보기 row; after a per-term undo, saving the dismissed exhibition deletes its record and leaves every derived count at zero, never negative, and the exhibition is unhidden. Written before the weights change (test-first); the owner's labels are the Assignment's second half.
9. Loading state that reads as work: replace the one-line "계산하고 있습니다" with the standard skeleton cards so the swap from featured to 내 취향 on Home is not a jump.

### Stage 2 — Talking (release 1.16, behind `ReleaseFeatures.TASTE_NARRATOR`; built only on the evidence trigger above)

10. `TasteNarrator` interface in `shared/commonMain` (`narrate(packet): NarratedTaste?`, `interpretAsk(text): AskFilters?`), a `TemplateTasteNarrator` in commonMain that produces today's strings, an Android implementation in `androidMain` on ML Kit GenAI Prompt API, and an iOS implementation written in Swift in `iosApp` on Foundation Models with guided generation, passed into `MainViewController` as a host parameter. Availability is checked at runtime (model present, device language Korean or English); otherwise the template serves. No `expect`/`actual`: interface plus injected implementation, the `NotificationScheduler` pattern.
11. Personal line at the top of 내 취향, two sentences, generated only from a packet of the ranker's evidence (taste terms with counts, anchor names, the top three picks with their reason kinds); validated with the same rule as Approach B (every exhibition name, artist and term in the output must appear in the packet; a Korean sentence must contain Hangul and any Latin run in it must be a substring of the packet; an English sentence may contain Hangul only as a substring of the packet; ≤120 characters per sentence); cached in memory by packet hash; discarded on any failure so the template basis line shows. No "AI" wording anywhere.
12. Natural-language ask in the List tab search field: a query like "비 오는 날 혼자 조용히" becomes structured filters (terms, districts, timing) through the narrator, with a deterministic synonym table as the fallback on every other device; the placeholder reads "전시명, 작가, 또는 '혼자 조용히 볼 전시'". The result is the ordinary filtered list, so the feature degrades to search, not to an error.
13. Weekly digest card on Home (Monday): "이번 주 당신을 위한 3편", same narrator, same validation; optional, after 11 and 12 are verified on devices.
